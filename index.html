The rules

Three decisions, applied consistently.

Tracking follows size

Letters read further apart as they grow, so display sizes pull in to -0.016em while body copy sits at zero and uppercase labels open to 0.12em. One letter-spacing value for the whole page is wrong somewhere.

Leading runs inverse

The hero sets at 1.0; a paragraph you actually read sets at 1.65. Tight leading looks deliberate on a headline and punishing in a paragraph.

Measure is a control

Paragraphs cap at 66ch by default, leads at 46ch. Line length is set by the type system, not by whatever width the container happened to be.

Usage

Reading, at length.

The two stylesheets are the system. tokens.css holds every family, size, leading, tracking and measure as a custom property; typography.css applies them to elements and exposes a short list of .t-* utilities. Nothing else in the project should contain a font-size.

Adding a section

Open with .t-eyebrow, then an h2, then a .t-lead if the section needs a sentence of orientation. Body copy needs no class — the element defaults already carry the right size, leading and measure.

  • .t-hero — one per page, above the fold.
  • .t-lead — sub-headline; caps at a 46ch measure.
  • .t-prose — long-form blocks; opens leading to 1.75.
  • .t-numeric — tabular figures for prices and tables.
  • .t-measure-* — override line length where 66ch is wrong.

Set the scale once, then spend your attention on the words.

The only rule worth enforcing

Changing the faces

Swap --font-display and --font-body in tokens.css and update the Google Fonts link in the <head>. If the new display face ships multiple weights, add a font-weight to the heading rule — Instrument Serif has one weight, which is why the headings don't set one and why font-synthesis-weight: none stops the browser faking a bold.
